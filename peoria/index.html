Typical Water Damage Problems in Peoria

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Peoria households. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. The low-cost defense is to swap out aging hoses and learn where the main shutoff is.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Peoria, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. Clean water from the tank or supply is one thing, but water from the bowl after use is contaminated. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Your air conditioner drains condensation through a line that most owners never see. A rusted pan or a plugged line lets that water run into nearby flooring or the ceiling below. Because it happens slowly, the damage in many Peoria homes is well along before a stain appears. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.
